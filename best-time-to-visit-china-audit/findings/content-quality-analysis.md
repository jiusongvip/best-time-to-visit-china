## E-E-A-T Breakdown

### Experience — 18/25 (72%)

**Strong signals:**
- Vivid narratives: "6:30 AM. You step onto the Great Wall at Mutianyu as the morning mist clears."
- Specific details: "jianbing — crispy crepe, egg, hoisin, 12 RMB"
- Regional specificity with distinct temperature ranges, avoidance windows, local tips
- Accurate festival timing that only comes from real scheduling knowledge

**Missing:**
- No photos or original imagery from claimed travels
- No named traveler behind the "Sarah/Mike/Anna" stories — feel fabricated
- No verification of "20+ cities" or "10-year weather data" claims

### Expertise — 8/25 (32%)

**Evidence of knowledge:**
- Accurate temperatures, rain days, crowd data across 12 months × 8 regions
- Correct festival dates (CNY, Mid-Autumn, Dragon Boat, Qing Ming)
- Practical visa advice reflects real 2024-2026 policy (72/144-hour transit)
- Cost data reasonably accurate for budget/mid/luxury tiers

**Missing:**
- **No author credentials** — not a single named person
- **No data sources cited** — which meteorological agency? What price tracking?
- **No citations or references** — zero links to official sources

### Authoritativeness — 5/25 (20%)

- No backlinks, no external citations, no media mentions
- No author platform (social media, travel forums, publications)
- "When to Visit China" has zero off-site footprint
- Essentially zero authority signals — a brand-new site with no external validation

### Trustworthiness — 10/30 (33%)

**Present:**
- HTTPS capable, updated dates visible, no deceptive ads/clickbait

**Missing:**
- No About page, Contact page, Privacy Policy, or Terms of Service
- No author identity — completely anonymous
- No corrections/update history
- No external verification — testimonials, reviews, media mentions all absent

## Key Findings

### Critical

1. **No author identity** — The single most damaging gap. December 2025 update penalizes anonymous content even for non-YMYL.

2. **Missing trust infrastructure** — No About, Contact, Privacy, or Terms pages.

3. **Unverifiable claims** — "10-year weather data," "20+ cities," "flight price tracking" — zero supporting evidence.

### High

4. **No visual proof** — Travel site without photography has inherently limited experience signals.

5. **Template-like testimonials** — Sarah/Mike/Anna stories follow identical structure, feel fabricated.

6. **No external citations** — Zero links to official sources that could validate data.

### Medium

7. **when-to-go.astro is thin** — ~400 words, purely a quiz funnel. Needs standalone content value.

8. **Static update date** — "Updated Jul 2026" is hardcoded. Individual data points need per-item freshness.

9. **No multimedia** — Zero images, videos, or infographics on a travel content site.

---

## Recommendations (Priority Order)

### Immediate
1. Create **About page** — name author(s), describe credentials, explain methodology
2. Add **author bylines + Person schema** on all content pages
3. Create **Contact page** and **Privacy Policy**
4. Add **data source citations** (e.g., "Temperature data from CMA, 2015-2025")

### Short-term
5. Add **real travel photography** — destination images, seasonal shots
6. Replace fabricated testimonials with **genuine traveler quotes** (or remove section)
7. Link to author's **verifiable external profiles**

### Medium-term
8. Create **llms.txt** for AI discovery
9. Expand **when-to-go.astro** with standalone content
10. Build **backlinks** — travel blogs, China forums, Reddit

**Bottom line:** The content itself is genuinely good — the kind Google claims to reward. But the complete absence of author identity, trust infrastructure, and verifiable credentials is a severe liability. **The fix is not content quality — it's content attribution.** Name who wrote this. Show why they know. Prove they went there.
